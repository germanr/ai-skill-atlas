# Creativity, ideation, and homogenization: literature screening, October 2026

## Scope and decision rule

This is a screening report, not a dataset update. It covers evidence on how generative AI changes human creative work: the rated creativity of what a person produces with AI at hand (`with_ai`), how alike different people's outputs become when they use AI (`across_people`), and what happens to unassisted creative work after a period of AI use (`after_ai`). The codebook (`docs/creativity-codebook.md`) fixes the vocabulary. No paper or estimate was added to the source files, the generated JSON, or the site. The site's creativity preview, which is hidden from the navigation, still holds the May 2026 collection.

The working eligibility rule for the core dataset has three criteria. People were randomly assigned to conditions that differ in generative-AI assistance. At least 50 people were analyzed across the arms of one randomized study. Creativity or diversity was scored on the output the participants produced, by raters or by a computed measure, so self-reports do not count. Papers that compare AI-generated output with human-generated output, observational studies of real output, and syntheses stay on the site as labeled context and never enter a pooled estimate. Randomized experiments with fewer than 50 people, non-random designs, model-only studies, theory papers, and studies that only count how much AI text exists are listed here and left out of the dataset.

"Unverified" in this report means that a required detail was not established from the material we could read, not that the study fails the criterion. "Needs clarification" means that a criterion rests on a passage the paper does not contain, usually how people came to be in each condition, or that a checker found a reason to doubt it. Author labels for papers that were not screened in full (experiments without full text and works excluded at triage) come from the search records and were not checked. Every other label was checked against Crossref, OpenAlex, arXiv, OSF, or the publisher.

## How the screening was done

**Search.** The search ran {{n_lanes}} agents, each on one source or angle and blind to the others: economics working papers, management, psychology and creativity journals, education, human-computer interaction, general science, recent arXiv, three observational domains, reference lists of reviews, replications and comments, AI-design contrasts, professionals in the field, writing and homogenization, design and engineering, and work after AI use. Four seed lists came first: the May 2026 collection, the included-study lists of three meta-analyses, the works that cite eight anchor experiments in OpenAlex, and the leads from a first scan. The lanes ran {{n_queries}} queries. With the seed lists they returned {{n_records}} records, and after linking versions of one work by DOI, arXiv id, listed other versions, and near-identical titles, {{n_works}} works remained, each with a stable id (`c001` to `c{{max_id}}`).

**Triage.** Two agents read each abstract independently and called it an experiment, context (AI versus human output, observational, or synthesis), or out, with the participant count and whether random assignment was stated. A work left the pipeline only when both agreed on "out". The {{n_library}} papers of the May collection went straight to the two-screener pipeline, and the other {{n_seed_triaged}} works from the seed lists were triaged. Of the {{n_lane_works}} works that only the search lanes found, the {{n_lane_triaged}} whose search class was an experiment of any kind were triaged. The other {{n_untriaged}} are listed in `docs/creativity-screening/identified-not-screened.md`.

**Full text.** A script tried arXiv, OSF, publisher PDF patterns, OpenAlex open-access locations, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC, the PDF link on the DOI landing page, and an arXiv title search. Retrieval agents then searched preprint servers, repositories, and author pages for what the script could not fetch, under a hard rule against pirate sources, credentials, and bot-check evasion. Germán downloaded {{n_owner}} papers by hand. Every PDF was converted to markdown with pymupdf4llm, and both files sit in `Research/ai-lab/literature/creativity/`.

**Two-screener pipeline.** For each experiment with full text, two agents filled the same form independently with different reading orders: one from the text to the tables, the other from the tables to the text. A reconciler listed every difference, settled each against the source with a quote and a page or table, and confirmed the eligibility facts even where the screeners agreed. For papers headed for the core, three challengers each tried to refute one criterion from the source (assignment, people, outcome). A finalizer then accepted or rejected each challenge against the source and stated the final verdict on the three criteria. A citation checker confirmed authors, title, year, venue, DOI, and versions against Crossref, OpenAlex, arXiv, OSF, or the publisher.

**Short-form pipeline.** Context papers, experiments under 50 people, designs that the abstract called non-random, and experiments whose abstract did not say how people were assigned went through one screener, an independent checker who found every passage again, a fixer when the checker disagreed, and the same citation check. The checker flags any paper that turns out to randomize people. Flagged papers with 50 or more people analyzed then went through the two-screener pipeline.

**Who did the work.** The search, the triage, and the first screening rounds ran as Claude Fable 5.1 agents on 4 to 6 October 2026. The finishing rounds, the short-form checks, and the experiments with unstated assignment ran as Codex jobs (gpt-6-astra) on 8 and 9 October, {{n_codex_jobs}} jobs in all. Every judgment is a JSON file under `docs/creativity-screening/forms/` with the quote and locator behind it, so a research assistant can trace each cell of this report to a page. No human has yet verified the forms.

**Audit.** After the report was assembled, Codex jobs audited it in {{n_audit_rounds}} rounds. In each round, finder jobs checked one part of the report against the forms, the source texts, and the registries: the prose, the coverage counts, the links, the tables, the status of each paper, the passages behind each recommended paper, and the author labels. A verifier job then checked each finding again on its own. We addressed every confirmed finding at its source (a form, a citation file, the report builder, or this text) and rebuilt the report. The rounds confirmed {{audit_confirmed_by_round}} findings, in that order. The audit records are in `docs/creativity-screening/audit/`.

## Coverage limits

Every search lane ran into the anonymous daily request budget of OpenAlex, so citation chasing and several keyword passes used Crossref, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC, arXiv, and web search instead. Crossref carries no abstracts for Elsevier journals, and Semantic Scholar rate-limited some citation lists. ScienceDirect, Taylor and Francis, SAGE, INFORMS, Wiley, and Springer answered scripted requests with HTTP 403 or a JavaScript bot check. Papers in those venues were confirmed from Crossref or PubMed records, and their full texts must be downloaded by hand. The lanes ran on 4 and 5 October 2026 and cover work posted up to then.

Several candidates are trial registrations with no paper yet, for example the AEA registry entries of Contreras and Leatherbee, Heshmati and Li, Huang, Long, and Pi, and Zissimos, Markoulakis, and Maeda. They are listed so that the next screening checks them again.

## The diversity measure: how it could be displayed

Three ways to show these effects on the site, with what each would display:

1. **A pooled standardized effect with a stated approximation.** Every similarity or diversity measure is converted to a standardized mean difference between arms, with a standard error that treats the paper's unit of analysis as independent. This gives one number per question and a forest plot in the learning section's format. It also pools measures that differ (cosine similarity of embeddings, semantic distance between ideas, rater-judged uniqueness, entropy of themes), and a standard error computed over pairs of outputs understates the uncertainty.
2. **A forest plot of standardized effects with no pooled number.** The same conversion and the same plot, without the summary row, and a caption that says why. Readers see the direction and the approximate size for every study without a headline number that depends on the conversion.
3. **Each paper's own measure with direction only.** A table that lists the measure, the unit, the reported values, and whether AI made outputs more alike or less alike, with no conversion. Nothing is approximated, and nothing can be pooled.

Two syntheses of the diversity question show why the choice matters. Holzner, Maier, and Feuerriegel (2025) pool six effect sizes from four studies and report that AI makes ideas less diverse, g = −0.86 [−1.33, −0.40]. De Rooij and Biskjaer (2026) pool 19 studies of the same question and report a homogenization effect of d = 0.33 [0.09, 0.57]. Both find that AI makes ideas more alike, but the magnitudes differ by a factor of about 2.6. The two syntheses include different studies and standardize different measures, so a pooled number depends on these choices.

## Decisions for Review 1

1. Which recommended papers enter, and which of the papers that need clarification we ask the authors about first.
2. Whether the 50-participant rule holds. The screening found {{n_small}} randomized experiments below the threshold. Their analyzed samples range from {{small_min}} to {{small_max}} people, and {{n_small_unreported}} of them have no analyzed count recorded. The rule also excludes Anderson, Shah, and Kreminski (2024) and Padmakumar and He (2024) from the May collection.
3. How to display diversity effects. The section on the diversity measure sets out three options and what each would show.
4. The standard-error rule when a paper computes its precision over ideas, texts, or pairs of outputs instead of people, and the order of preferred standardizers.
5. Which result leads the page: creativity with AI together with diversity across people, or creativity after AI.
6. Whether the site hosts PDFs for creativity papers. Proposed rule: link to the DOI or arXiv page, and host a PDF only when the paper is open access.
7. Whether Contractor and Reyes (2026) enters as an `across_people` record, flagged as our own paper. It would appear in both sections.
8. Whether exposure to AI-generated ideas, with no interaction with a model, counts as generative-AI assistance. This decides the `ideas_supplied` role, which covers Doshi and Hauser (2024) and Ashkinaze et al. (2025) among others.
9. How to treat experiments that randomize intact classes or teams with few clusters, for example Abu Asba and Al-Rababah (2026) with three classes, and the class-level lotteries in several education papers.
10. Whether a writing-quality score counts as usefulness. Gero et al. (2026) would become core if an IELTS-based writing-quality composite counts.
11. Whether within-person designs that randomize the order of conditions enter the core under the same 50-person rule.
12. Two recommended papers sit at the edge of the codebook's scope, and their forms leave the call to Review 1. McGuire, De Cremer, and Van de Cruys (2024) use a 2020 neural poetry generator that is not a large language model. Qin et al. (2025) ask writers for supporting arguments for a given health claim and score them as ideas.

## Implications for extraction

Record randomized, analyzed, and unique-participant counts separately, together with the number of clusters when assignment was by class or team. For each outcome, record who or what scored it, the unit of the paper's own analysis, whether AI was available when the output was produced, the delay for after-AI outcomes, and for diversity measures the metric, what it was computed over, and how its uncertainty was obtained. Record participant overlap across papers (Meincke, Nave, and Terwiesch 2025 reanalyze the participants of Lee and Chung 2024) and company involvement (the two Dell'Acqua et al. field experiments ran with Boston Consulting Group and with Procter and Gamble). Keep the version read, the latest version, and the retrieval date as separate fields, because many papers exist as a preprint and a journal article with different samples.
