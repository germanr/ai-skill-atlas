# Citation check of 33 leads from the first scan (2026-10-04)

Search input for the creativity screening. Produced by a read-only sub-agent; every entry is rechecked downstream.

I located all 33 entries in primary records. 18 are confirmed as given (2 of those with scope caveats), 13 needed corrections or updates, and 2 needed sources filled in (7, 16). The rest of this message covers what I checked and the entries whose errors matter. Sources were the arXiv API, Crossref, OpenAlex, PubMed, NBER, IZA, RePEc, OSF, the Tilburg portal and a mirror of the 2023 working paper. ScienceDirect, INFORMS, HBS and Nature blocked direct access (HTTP 403), so for those I used Crossref, PubMed or RePEc records. Sample sizes are reported only where the abstract states them. I created no files.

1. **Confirmed.** Kumar, H., Vincentius, J., Jordan, E., & Anderson, A. (2025). "Human Creativity in the Age of LLMs: Randomized Experiments on Divergent and Convergent Thinking." CHI '25 Proceedings, 1–18. doi:10.1145/3706598.3714198; arXiv 2410.03703. Design: participants randomized to a standard LLM, a coach-like LLM, or no AI, followed by a final unassisted stage; two preregistered parallel experiments. N = 1,100. Nothing corrected.
2. **Confirmed.** Dong, M., & Yakura, H. (2026). "Human diversity fuels collective creativity that large language models cannot simulate or sustain." arXiv 2607.26899 (preprint). Design: preregistered metaphor-writing experiment with native and non-native English writers in three arms (no AI, AI ideation, AI refinement). It also simulates the writer pool with LLM personas. N not stated. Nothing corrected.
3. **Corrected.** Dell'Acqua, F., et al. (11 authors) (2026). "The Cybernetic Teammate: A Field Experiment on Generative AI and Teamwork." Organization Science 37(4), 1217–1242. doi:10.1287/orsc.2025.20702. Earlier version: NBER WP 33641 (April 2025), titled "…Generative AI Reshaping Teamwork and Expertise," doi:10.3386/w33641. Design: preregistered field experiment; professionals randomized to AI or no AI, working alone or in two-person teams. N = 791 in the published abstract; 776 is the NBER figure. Corrected: sample size for the published version; volume and pages added.
4. **Corrected.** Dell'Acqua, F., et al. (9 authors) (2026). "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality." Organization Science 37(2), 403–423. doi:10.1287/orsc.2025.21838. Earlier version: HBS WP 24-013 / SSRN 4573321 (2023), with "AI" in the title. Design: preregistered experiment; 758 consultants randomized to no AI, GPT-4, or GPT-4 plus a prompt-engineering overview.
   - **Variability:** yes. The 2023 working paper reports "a marked reduction in the variability of these ideas compared to those not using AI" for the shoe-idea task (body text, Fig. 6, App. D). It is not in either abstract. I could not open the published full text to confirm the passage is still there.
   - Corrected: it is now published, and the published title spells out "Artificial Intelligence."
5. **Corrected.** Zhou, Y., Liu, Q., Huang, J., & Li, G. (2026; online Sept 2025). "Creative scar without generative AI: Individual creativity fails to sustain while homogeneity keeps climbing." Technology in Society 84, 103087. doi:10.1016/j.techsoc.2025.103087.
   - Design: Study 1 is a natural experiment on 419,344 Web of Science papers before and after ChatGPT-3.5. Study 2 is a 7-day lab experiment with two follow-ups, in which half the participants used ChatGPT-4 (3,593 ideas, 427 solutions). Participant N not stated.
   - Corrected: Zhou is first author. arXiv 2401.06816 is a precursor with a different title ("When ChatGPT is gone: Creativity reverts and homogeneity persists"), with Liu first, 61 students and 3,302 ideas.

CHI '25 Proceedings, 1–21. doi:10.1145/3706598.3713564; arXiv 2409.11360. Design: controlled experiment, writing with vs. without AI suggestions. N = 118 (India and the US). Nothing corrected.
